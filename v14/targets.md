# A family of targets, a reference, and Sanov's rate

> **Status: draft of v14 (Q69); proved here, not yet checked in CI. TG1's reading of a family is the executor's
> proposal, not a decision.** The numbers come from `probes/v14/resolution.py` (`probes/v14/RESULTS-resolution.md`).

v13 let a principal accept a whole **family of targets** `𝓚` ([D3]): a closed set of objectives `G : X → ℝ` that holds
the constants and every combination `a·G + b·G'`, `a, b ≥ 0`, of its members: one objective with its positive
rescalings, a ranking's increasing functions, or a span of candidates. v13 read the family through optimization: its
intended behaviours were the pursuits `tilt(q, G)`, `G ∈ 𝓚`, of its targets from a default `q`. v14's CORE has no
optimization, so it needs another reading. This file proposes one, compares it with v13's, and gives the large-deviation
reading of misalignment, Sanov's rate, which holds whatever the acceptable set.

### TG1 — A family of targets and a reference give a list of budgets
**Statement.** Let `𝓚` be a family of targets and `r ∈ Δ` a reference. Let `D_𝓚(r) = {a ∈ Δ : E_a[G] ≥ E_r[G]` for
every `G ∈ 𝓚}`, the behaviours **no worse than the reference on any target of the family**.
(i) `D_𝓚(r)` is convex and closed, and contains `r`.
(ii) If `𝓚` is generated by the constants and `G_1, …, G_m`, its members being `c + Σ_i λ_i·G_i` with `λ_i ≥ 0`, then
`D_𝓚(r) = ∩_i {a : E_a[G_i] ≥ E_r[G_i]}`: `m` budgets, `(−G_i, −E_r[G_i])` in `constraints.md`'s form.
(iii) For the increasing functions of a ranking, `D_𝓚(r)` is `order.md`'s `B(r)`, better for every reading (VO1); for
`{t·F + c : t ≥ 0}`, it is the single budget `E_a[F] ≥ E_r[F]`; for a span, `±T_i ∈ 𝓚`, it fixes the averages of every
`T_i` at the reference's.
(iv) If `−1_C ∈ 𝓚` and `r(C) = 0`, every behaviour of `D_𝓚(r)` gives `C` mass `0`: the family and the reference
together forbid `C`, with no constraint declared.

**In plain terms.** A principal who accepts any target of a family, and holds a reference, asks for a behaviour that
does no worse than the reference on every target it might hold. That is a list of limits on averages, one for each
target that generates the family, so everything `constraints.md` and `evidence.md` prove applies to it. A ranking is the
family of its increasing scores, and the requirement is then dominance.

**Proof.** (i) Each `{a : E_a[G] ≥ E_r[G]}` is a closed half-space met with `Δ`, and contains `r`; an intersection of
such sets is convex, closed, and contains `r`. (ii) For `G = c + Σ_i λ_i·G_i`, `E_a[G] − E_r[G] = Σ_i λ_i·(E_a[G_i] −
E_r[G_i])`, non-negative when every term is. (iii) VO1 for the ranking; the ray has one generator, `F`; for a span,
`T_i` and `−T_i` both give budgets. (iv) `E_a[−1_C] ≥ −r(C) = 0` is `a(C) ≤ 0`. ∎

**Notes.** A budget `(−G_i, −E_r[G_i])` needs `−E_r[G_i] > min(−G_i)`, that is, `E_r[G_i] < max G_i`; when the reference
already does as well as possible on `G_i`, the budget is a forbidden set in disguise (`constraints.md`), as in (iv). The
reading is the one VO1 gives an order, extended to any family: unanimity over the targets the principal might hold,
against a reference. It needs a reference; a principal with none states absolute requirements, constraints, instead.

### TG2 — Doing no worse on every target is not pursuing one of them
**Statement.** Let `q ∈ Δ°` and `𝓘_𝓚 = {tilt(q, G) : G ∈ 𝓚}`, v13's intended behaviours for `𝓚` ([D3]).
(i) For `𝓚 = {t·F + c : t ≥ 0}`, `𝓘_𝓚 ⊆ D_𝓚(q)`: every pursuit of `F` does at least as well on `F` as the default.
(ii) For the increasing functions of a total ranking by `F`, `𝓘_𝓚 ⊆ D_𝓚(q) = B(q)` (VO2).
(iii) If `G_1, G_2 ∈ 𝓚` have `Cov_q(G_1, G_2) < 0`, then for every small enough `t > 0`, `tilt(q, t·G_1) ∈ 𝓘_𝓚` lies
outside `D_𝓚(q)`: pursuing one target of the family does worse than the default on another.
(iv) For `p̂ ∈ Δ°`, v13's misalignment against `𝓘_𝓚` is `KL(p̂‖q) − min_{p ∈ D_𝓚(p̂)} KL(p‖q)`: the departure from the
default beyond the cheapest behaviour no worse than the actor itself on any target ([P53](iv)).

**In plain terms.** In v13, a principal who accepted several candidate targets accepted an actor that pursued any one of
them, even at another's expense. Read as a requirement, the same family asks for no loss on any of them. For one target,
and for a ranking, the two point the same way, the pursuits lying inside the requirement. With targets in tension they
part: pursuing helpfulness may cost harmlessness, and v13 accepted that while TG1 does not. v13's measure was itself a
comparison with a requirement of this form, with the actor as its own reference.

**Proof.** (i) `t ↦ E_{tilt(q,t·F)}[F]` has derivative `Var_{tilt(q,t·F)}(F) ≥ 0`, so its value at `t ≥ 0` is at least
its value `E_q[F]` at `0`; constants change no average. (ii) is VO2. (iii) `t ↦ E_{tilt(q,t·G_1)}[G_2]` has derivative
`Cov_{tilt(q,t·G_1)}(G_1, G_2)`, which is continuous in `t` and negative at `0`, so the average falls below `E_q[G_2]`
for small `t > 0`, and `G_2 ∈ 𝓚` makes that a breach of `D_𝓚(q)`. (iv) The set `C` of [P53](iv), the behaviours that do
at least as well as `p̂` on every target, is `D_𝓚(p̂)`; [P53](iv) is the formula. ∎

**Notes.** For four outcomes, a uniform default, `G_1 = (0, 1, 2, 3)` and `G_2 = (3, 2, 0, 1)`, with covariance `−1.0`
under `q`, the pursuit of `G_1` at intensity `0.5` raises `E[G_1]` from `1.5` to `2.08` and lowers `E[G_2]` from `1.5`
to `1.09`; and (iv) holds within `1e-13` on 30 random two-target cones (`RESULTS-resolution.md`). Which reading CORE
takes is the PI's decision; the executor recommends TG1's, because it needs no model of optimization, is convex, and
makes v13's own measure a special case of comparing with a reference.

### SN1 — Sanov's rate, the best bet and the nearest acceptable behaviour are one number
**Statement.** Let `A ⊆ Δ` be non-empty, `n ≥ 1`, and `k_n = (n + |X| − 1)!/(n!·(|X| − 1)!)`.
(i) For every empirical behaviour `s` of `n` decisions, `e^{−n·M_A(s)}/k_n ≤ sup_{a∈A} P_a(s) ≤ e^{−n·M_A(s)}`, where
`P_a(s)` is the probability that `n` independent decisions drawn from `a` have shares `s`. So
`(1/n)·log sup_{a∈A} P_a(s) = −M_A(s) + O(log n/n)`, uniformly in `s`.
(ii) If `A` is convex and closed, the same number is the growth rate of the best bet against `A` (VE1), and a nearest
acceptable behaviour `a*` is a most likely one to have produced the counts, not always unique: the best bet is the
likelihood ratio `s/a*`, fitted to the record, not fixed in advance.
(iii) If `A` is not convex, the bets against `A` are those against its hull (VE2), so the best bet grows at
`M_{conv A}(s) ≤ M_A(s)`: the rate at which counts become improbable under every acceptable behaviour can exceed the
rate any bet reaches, by what a schedule of acceptable behaviours explains.

**In plain terms.** Misalignment has three readings that coincide on convex requirements: how fast a record becomes
improbable for the acceptable behaviour that explains it best (Sanov's rate, a large deviation); how fast the best fair
bet against acceptance grows (Kelly); and how far, in KL, the actor is from the nearest acceptable behaviour (an
information projection). They part only when the requirement is not convex, where counts charge an actor that switches
between acceptable behaviours and bets do not.

**Proof.** (i) is [P55](i), whose proof uses no property of the set: for each `a ∈ Δ`, `P_a(s) ≤ e^{−n·KL(s‖a)}` and
`P_a(s) ≥ e^{−n·KL(s‖a)}/k_n`; take the supremum over `a ∈ A`. Since `k_n ≤ (n + 1)^{|X|−1}`, the error is at most
`(|X| − 1)·log(n + 1)/n`. (ii) VE1, and `P_a(s)` is, up to the multinomial coefficient, `e^{−n·(KL(s‖a) + H(s))}`, with
`H(s)` the entropy of `s`, largest where `KL(s‖a)` is least. (iii) VE2(i). ∎

**Notes.** This is the method of types, Sanov's theorem in the form a composite hypothesis needs, as [A4] and [P55] use
it. For four outcomes and one budget, the logarithm of the largest probability lies between the two bounds at
`n = 20, 100, 500`; for two acceptable behaviours and an actor at their midpoint, Sanov's rate against the pair is
`0.331` nats per decision and the best bet's growth, against their hull, is `0` (`RESULTS-resolution.md`). The other
order of the arguments, `KL(a‖p̂)`, is Stein's exponent, and answers another question (`evidence.md`, why this measure).
