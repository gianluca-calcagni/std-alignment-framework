# M3 — Imported theorems
> [!info] Generated from [CORE.md](https://github.com/gianluca-calcagni/std-alignment-framework/blob/main/CORE.md#m3--imported-theorems). Edit the source, not this note.

## Statement
Besides finite sums and [[M2 — Independent repetition|M2]], the results of `derived/` use these theorems: Gibbs' and Pinsker's
inequalities; the Neyman–Pearson lemma; the central limit theorem and the delta method; Wilks's theorem; Chernoff's
theorem; Huber's limit law for estimates that solve an equation; and Čencov's theorem, which gives one reason for the
definition of pursuit (section 2) and nothing a result rests on.

## In plain terms
The framework borrows a short list of standard theorems, each checked in a text anyone can read,
and assumes nothing else from outside.

## Status
Imported, each with its hypotheses quoted where a result uses it, and read in a text anyone can read without
paying, or derived where it is used; `REFERENCES.md` says where each is read.

## Why this choice
Proving classical theorems again adds nothing a reader can check better in their source. Quoting
their hypotheses where they are used keeps each result honest about what it needs, and listing them here makes the
boundary between what the framework assumes and what it borrows visible at once.
