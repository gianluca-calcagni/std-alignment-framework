# Derived — estimation and evidence

What samples ([[D11 — Record, sample and evidence|D11]]) reveal about misalignment. The expected evidence per decision is a KL divergence, so misalignment
is the slowest rate at which evidence against the specification accumulates ([[P21 — The expected evidence is misalignment|P21]]); no test separates an actor from its
nearest intended behaviour faster ([[P22 — No test detects misalignment faster than misalignment|P22]]); the estimated misalignment of an actor that does pursue the objective has a
known distribution ([[P23 — The estimated misalignment of an actor that pursues the objective|P23]]); and an evaluation that weights conditions differently from deployment sees a different
misalignment, by a computable gap ([[P24 — The evaluation gap|P24]]). [[P22 — No test detects misalignment faster than misalignment|P22]] and [[P23 — The estimated misalignment of an actor that pursues the objective|P23]] rest on classical theorems, quoted with their hypotheses. A
strong incentive makes actors indistinguishable where it applies, which can make an evaluation see no misalignment while
use sees the actor's own ([[P37 — A strong incentive masks the actor, and can fake alignment|P37]]). What a sample certifies about an actor off the ray depends on what is known of each
draw: counted outcomes, log-ratios against the default, or log-ratios with draws of the default each give a normal law
with its own variance, and near the ray the second is far sharper ([[P52 — What a sample certifies about misalignment, by access|P52]]). An actor whose decisions depend on each
other, or drift, gives more evidence than its counts show, by exactly two terms, so the counts judge its average
behaviour ([[P54 — Dependence and drift only add evidence|P54]]). And whatever the specification, counts certify misalignment by a test exact at every size, with an
interval of the same kind ([[P55 — What a record of counts certifies, at every size|P55]]).
