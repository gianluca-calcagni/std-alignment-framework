# Derived — the evaluator

What an evaluator ([[D10 — Evaluator, regression and residual|D10]]) does to the principal's objective when the actor pursues it. Through the evaluator, only the
regression of the target on it counts ([[P18 — Through the evaluator, only the regression counts|P18]]). A regression that rises with the evaluator rules out overoptimization
([[P19 — A monotone regression rules out overoptimization|P19]]). Otherwise, overoptimization starts where the evaluator stops telling anything about the target under the
current behaviour, and at high intensity it is decided by the evaluator's two highest values ([[P20 — Where overoptimization starts, and how it ends|P20]]). The target's curve
turns no more often than the regression does, so a single-peaked regression gives at most one fall ([[P25 — The target's curve turns no more often than the regression|P25]]). For an
evaluator that scores every outcome differently, the regression on bins of its values governs the pursuit while the
intensity is small against the bins ([[P26 — The regression on bins governs at small intensity|P26]]). For an evaluator known in the target's units, pursued within a
departure budget, the target lost is at most the width of the budget along the evaluator's error, and that bound is
the exact worst case ([[P29 — The width is the exact worst case|P29]]); which of two errors is worse depends on the budget, so no bound that separates the error
from the budget can be accurate at every budget ([[L1 — Separable bounds are loose when two quantities change rank|L1]], [[P30 — No separable bound on the worst case|P30]]). Computable bounds on misalignment and on the width
follow from the error's range and tails ([[P33 — Error bounds for a known evaluator|P33]]); and when the evaluator and the target choose from the same
candidates, the loss is at most the error's spread over them, draw by draw, which is again the exact worst case ([[P34 — Choosing by the evaluator from a common candidate set|P34]]).
