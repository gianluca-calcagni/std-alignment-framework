# General results — a dictionary from finite to continuous outcomes

Each object of the framework, written three ways: as the finite core states it (`CORE.md`, `derived/`), as the general
core states it (`CORE-GENERAL.md`, `transfer.md`), and as the imported sources treat it when they treat the continuous
case at all. Asked by the PI (Q34), to see what changes between the two, and to prepare the general core's draft 4
(`ROADMAP.md`, step 3). Nothing here is claimed: the general column has the statuses of `README.md` in this folder, and
a source is marked **read** when its setting and the theorem cited were read in its text (arXiv, 2026-10-07), and
**abstract** when only its record and abstract were (`NOTES.md` §3.2, D12).

## 2. What the imports say about the continuous case

1. **Alignment theory mostly stays finite, on purpose.** The tampering, influenceable-reward and elicitation sources
   assume finite sets and say why: they "can all be chosen very large". The finite core made the same choice ([[D1 — Outcomes, behaviours, divergence and tilt|D1]]). The
   continuum enters alignment through two doors only: tails ([[References|@kwa2024]]) and compactness ([[References|@zhuang2020]]).
2. **One function decides most of the continuous case: the log-normalizer `Λ(t) = log E_q[e^{t·F}]`.** Where it is
   finite, tilts exist (GD2), and the default is light-tailed in Kwa et al.'s sense; its convex dual gives the frontier
   of stakes (GD5) and the worst case over a KL ball, as in robust optimization [[References|@bental2013]]; and the tilts it allows
   are the exponential manifold of Pistone and Sempi [[References|@pistone1995]]. On finite outcomes `Λ` is finite everywhere, so
   these coincide without being noticed.
3. **The imports pass to the continuum the way GA6 does:** by finite descriptions. Mutual information is the supremum
   over binnings ([[References|@mcallester2020]]), the divergence the supremum over partitions ([[References|@pinsker1964]]), Blackwell's
   comparisons drop finiteness ([[References|@blackwell1953]]), and the best-of-`n` formula is the limit as atoms vanish
   ([[References|@beirami2024]]).
4. **Atoms are the hinge.** A formula for a continuum is the limit of finite ones as every outcome's probability goes to
   zero (best-of-`n`, the regression of [[D10 — Evaluator, regression and residual|D10]]). The other way round, an actor that creates an atom, a value pinned that
   the default never pins, is infinitely misaligned (GD3), cannot be a pass-through (GD6), and tampers infinitely with
   an atomless channel (§1).
5. **Existence becomes conditional.** On finite outcomes every minimum is attained. On a continuum each needs a
   compactness or a closedness: attainment of misalignment ([[P5 — Misalignment is attained, and zero exactly on the intended behaviours and their limits|P5]], `transfer.md`), Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell's compact
   upper level sets, Lindsay's compact likelihood trace, Csiszár's sets closed in variation.
6. **Bounds that are finite become infinite.** The most tampering consistent with signals (§1), the frontier when
   `t_max = 0`, and misalignment for pinned values. In each case the finite core's bound was finite only because the
   outcome space was.
7. **Estimation depends on access, not on the space.** Plug-in estimation fails on a continuum (GD11), and what samples
   can certify depends on which behaviour is sampled and which is known. There are four regimes:
   - the log-ratio is known for each draw, as for language models scored by both policies: the core's quantities are
     Monte Carlo averages, as in cases W3 and W4, and reweighting needs about `e^{KL}` draws [[References|@chatterjee2018]];
   - the actor is sampled and the intended behaviour known: no sample certifies that misalignment is small (GD11),
     though a record at a declared resolution certifies it is at least its value there;
   - the reference is sampled and the other behaviour known: no distribution-free lower bound above `ln N`
     [[References|@mcallester2020]];
   - both are only sampled: both limits apply.
   So the engineering question for continuous outcomes is which log-ratios a case can compute, more than which space its
   outcomes live in.
